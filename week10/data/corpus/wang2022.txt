Wang et al. (2022), Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models
Self-consistency replaces greedy decoding in chain-of-thought prompting with a sample-and-vote procedure: several reasoning paths are sampled from the model at a non-zero temperature, and the final answer is chosen by majority vote over the answers those paths reach. The intuition is that a complex problem admits several different reasoning routes to the same correct answer, while wrong routes tend to disagree with each other.
Across four large language models the method improved accuracy by a striking margin on arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks: +17.9% on GSM8K, +11.0% on SVAMP, +12.2% on AQuA, +6.4% on StrategyQA and +3.9% on ARC-challenge. It is entirely unsupervised and needs no extra training, at the cost of several samples per question.
