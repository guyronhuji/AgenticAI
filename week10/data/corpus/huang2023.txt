Huang et al. (2023), Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet
This paper examines intrinsic self-correction: asking a language model to review and revise its own reasoning without any external feedback, oracle labels or tools. Across reasoning benchmarks the authors find that such self-correction does not improve accuracy and frequently degrades it, because the model changes correct answers to incorrect ones about as often as the reverse when it is prompted to reconsider.
Earlier positive results, the paper argues, relied on oracle information such as knowing when the first answer was wrong, or on external feedback. The conclusion is that reliable correction requires a new signal, for example the result of executing code or a check against an external source, rather than a second pass over the same information.
