Kaplan et al. (2020), Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models
This study finds that the cross-entropy loss of Transformer language models follows smooth power laws in three quantities: model size (non-embedding parameters), dataset size, and the amount of compute used for training, each spanning more than six orders of magnitude. Other architectural details such as width versus depth have only a weak effect within a wide range.
The power laws make it possible to predict the loss of larger models from smaller ones and to allocate a fixed compute budget: the authors conclude that larger models are significantly more sample-efficient, so that compute-efficient training uses very large models trained on a relatively modest amount of data, stopping well before convergence. Later work (Hoffmann et al., 2022) revised the recommended balance toward more data.
