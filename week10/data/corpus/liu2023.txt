Liu et al. (2023), Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts
This paper measures how well language models actually use the information in a long input context. In multi-document question answering and key-value retrieval experiments, performance is highest when the relevant information appears at the beginning or the end of the input, and it degrades significantly when the relevant information is in the middle of a long context, producing a characteristic U-shaped curve.
The effect appears even in models explicitly designed for long contexts, and performance decreases as the input grows. The practical consequence for retrieval-augmented systems is that simply stuffing more passages into the prompt does not reliably help; the position and number of retrieved passages matter.
