Lewis et al. (2020), Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) combines a pre-trained parametric sequence-to-sequence model (BART) with a non-parametric memory: a dense vector index of Wikipedia accessed through a neural retriever (DPR). At generation time the retriever returns passages conditioned on the input, and the generator conditions on both the input and the retrieved passages. Two formulations are compared: RAG-Sequence, which uses the same retrieved passages for the whole output, and RAG-Token, which can draw on different passages per token.
Fine-tuned end to end, RAG set the state of the art on three open-domain question answering tasks (Natural Questions, TriviaQA and WebQuestions) and generated language that was more specific, diverse and factual than a parametric-only baseline. The non-parametric memory can be swapped or updated without retraining the model.
