Yao et al. (2022), ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models
ReAct prompts a language model to interleave reasoning traces and task-specific actions: the model writes a Thought, emits an Action (such as a Wikipedia search), and receives an Observation from the environment, repeating until it finishes. Reasoning helps the model plan and handle exceptions; acting lets it gather information from external sources, which reduces hallucination and error propagation compared with chain-of-thought alone.
On question answering (HotpotQA) and fact verification (FEVER) ReAct outperforms reasoning-only and acting-only baselines and produces human-interpretable trajectories. On the interactive decision-making benchmarks ALFWorld and WebShop it beats imitation-learning and reinforcement-learning methods by 34% and 10% absolute success rate respectively, using only one or two in-context examples.
