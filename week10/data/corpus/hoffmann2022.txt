Hoffmann et al. (2022), Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models
This work asks how to split a fixed compute budget between model size and training data. By training over 400 models from 70 million to 16 billion parameters on 5 to 500 billion tokens, the authors find that current large models are substantially undertrained: for compute-optimal training the number of parameters and the number of training tokens should be scaled in equal proportion, roughly doubling both whenever compute increases fourfold.
To test the prediction they trained Chinchilla, a 70-billion-parameter model on 1.4 trillion tokens, using the same compute budget as the 280-billion-parameter Gopher. Chinchilla uniformly outperforms Gopher, GPT-3 (175B) and Megatron-Turing NLG (530B) on a large set of downstream tasks, reaching 67.5% average accuracy on the MMLU benchmark, and it is cheaper to run at inference time because it is smaller.
